00 · engine

The real-time engine: why nothing above is allowed to block

The OS calls the audio callback on a hard deadline — one buffer's worth of time. Miss it once and it drops audio: a glitch, not a slow frame. So the callback does exactly one thing: push or pop a lock-free queue. Detect, quantize, and shift all happen on a separate worker thread that has no deadline and is free to allocate, lock, or block.

why each one is fatal
blocka mutex, file I/O, or sleep — the callback simply doesn't return in time, no matter how briefly it was going to block for
allocatenew, a growing Vec/vector — the allocator can itself take an internal lock or walk a free list; worst case is unbounded, not "usually fast"
lockany mutex, even uncontended — if the other side is ever preempted while holding it, the audio thread stalls waiting on a thread the OS isn't scheduling

The detector needs a fixed-size block (2048 samples) to run its FFT against. Audio hosts don't guarantee one — a DAW can hand the callback 64, 128, or 512 samples depending on its own buffer setting, and that size can even change mid-session.

01 · detect

Finding the pitch: autocorrelation, normalized

A pitched sound is periodic — its waveform repeats every T samples, where T is the pitch period. That single fact is the entire idea: take a copy of the signal, slide it sideways by some lag τ, and ask "does this copy look like the original again?" For almost every τ the answer is no. But the moment τ happens to equal the true period T (or a whole multiple of it), the shifted copy lines back up with the original almost exactly — because that's what "periodic" means. So: try every possible τ, measure how well each shifted copy matches, and the τ with the best match (past the trivial τ=0 case) is the pitch period. That measuring is what autocorrelation is; normalizing the measurement so it's comparable at every τ is the McLeod Pitch Method's own contribution, covered further down.

the math
energy gateE = Σ x[n]² — skip the block entirely if E < 0.15
autocorrelationACF(τ) = IFFT( |FFT(x)|² ) — Wiener–Khinchin, O(N log N) instead of O(N²)
energy termm(τ) = Σ x[n]² + Σ x[n+τ]²  (left window energy + right window energy)
NSDFnsdf(τ) = 2·ACF(τ) / m(τ) — bounded to [−1, 1], comparable across every τ
peak choicefirst τ past the initial positive run where nsdf(τ) > 0.1 — not the tallest peak
sub-sample refinefit a parabola through (−1,y₀), (0,y₁), (1,y₂) around the peak, solve the vertex

02 · quantize

Snapping to the scale: outward search, ties go low

Twelve pitch classes, wrapped into an octave. If the detected note is already one of the scale's own notes, it passes through untouched. If not, the search walks outward by semitone — one below, one above, two below, two above — until it lands on one that belongs.

the math
pitch classpc(note) = ((note mod 12) + 12) mod 12 — the extra +12 handles negative notes safely
membershipif scale.contains(pc(note)): return note unchanged
search orderfor d in 1..6: check pc(note−d), then pc(note+d) — first match wins, below beats above on a tie

03 · shift

Three engines, three genuinely different mechanisms

Once the target note is known, something has to actually move the audio's pitch there. Pitchzazz ships three hot-swappable engines that solve this with unrelated physics: one reasons about frequency, one about time-domain grains, one about playback rate. All three get real windowing and real math below — this is where that shows up most.

Phase Vocoder — frequency domain

Take the signal into the frequency domain with an FFT, then move each bin's content to a new bin scaled by the pitch ratio. Relocating spectral content is the pitch shift; the phase math below exists purely to make that relocation sound coherent instead of smeared.

Live demo below: drag the slider — it recomputes bin′ = round(k · shift) for 12 source bins in real time and redraws where each one actually lands.
the math
shift ratioshift = 2^(semitoneShift / 12)
phase differenceΔφk = (φk[t] − φk[t−1]) − k·(2π/oversampling)
unwrap (qpd trick)fold Δφk into (−π, π] by subtracting the nearest even multiple of π
true frequencyωk = k·(2π/oversampling) + Δφk · oversampling — per-bin, per-frame
bin relocationbin′ = round(k · shift) — magnitude and ωk both move here
phase accumulationφ′k[t] = φ′k[t−1] + hop·ωk — persists across frames, the fix for "phasiness"

TD-PSOLA — time domain, grain-based

No FFT at all. Cut the signal into small windowed grains, one centered on each detected pitch period, then paste those grains back down at new positions — closer together to raise pitch, farther apart to lower it. Each grain's own content never changes, only its spacing does.

the math
synthesis spacingspacing = periodSamples / shiftRatio — denser for a shift up, sparser for a shift down
grain half-widthhalfWidth = periodSamples · grainWidthMultiplier — multiplier of the unshifted period, so formants survive
read positionreadMark = floor(synthesisMarkPos / periodSamples + ε) · periodSamples — floor, not round, to bound lookahead
latencylatencySamples = 2 · maxHalfWidthSamples — scales with pitch period, not a fixed window

Varispeed + WSOLA — stretch, then resample

The odd one out: this engine changes pitch by genuinely changing playback rate, the same physical effect as varispeed-ing a tape reel — formants shift right along with it, on purpose. Two stages, in this order: WSOLA time-stretches the signal by the shift ratio first (pitch-preserving, duration-changing), then a resample restores the original duration, which is what actually moves the pitch.

the math
splice scoreNCC(offset) = Σ(a·b) / √(Σa² · Σb²) — normalized cross-correlation, capped to a 256-sample window
deviate only ifNCC(offset) > NCC(0) + 0.02 — a hard margin, not a soft distance penalty
resamplep(t) = 0.5·[2p₁ + (p₂−p₀)t + (2p₀−5p₁+4p₂−p₃)t² + (3p₁−3p₂+p₃−p₀)t³]
read rateread position advances by shift = 2^(semitoneShift/12) samples per output sample

04 · hot-swap

Changing engines live, with no dropout

Switching from the phase vocoder to TD-PSOLA mid-performance can't pause audio to do it. The handoff is one atomic pointer exchange, picked up by the worker thread between blocks — never mid-process() — and the outgoing engine's warmed-up internal state gets cross-faded out instead of just discarded.

the math
both engines runfor crossfadeBlocks = 4 blocks (≈185ms @ 44.1kHz/2048), old and new both process the same input
equal-power blendgainOld = cos(θ), gainNew = sin(θ), θ ramped 0→π/2 continuously across the whole window
why not linearlinear (1−t)/t dips below full power mid-transition (gainOld²+gainNew² < 1); equal-power keeps the sum exactly 1 throughout
